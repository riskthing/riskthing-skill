# Risk title

The full rules behind RiskThing's in-app "refine title" helper for risks. Use them to write a new title or to refine a draft. Edit the rules and examples to fit the organization; the tests are the part to keep.

**Inputs:** the draft title, the risk description, the project context (`find_projects`) and what you know about the company.
**Output:** one title, for `title_public`. No explanation, no quotation marks.

A title is the one line most readers ever see of the row. A founder, a program manager or an engineer scans it in a list. It names what is at stake and what could happen to it, in the shorthand the company's own people would use if they wrote the line themselves.

## What a good title is

Two things, in this order: **the asset at stake**, then **what could happen to it**. The asset is something the company has or wants: revenue, a sale, a flight, a delivery date, a claim it makes, its credibility with a buyer or an investor, a part, a page. The departure is the way that asset could be lost, delayed, challenged or spent. The condition that makes it real belongs in the description, unless it fits in a short trailing "because" or "with" phrase.

Either a noun phrase or a short plain sentence. Both are fine.

- Continued federal grant revenue after the ORBIT-2 Phase II ends in May 2027
- GEO sales lost to a worst-case radiation assumption, because the Halo 6000 control path names no part class
- The 2022 and 2025 Form Ds list different leadership than the website

Under 140 characters always; under 100 when the asset and the departure fit. Every word is spent on the asset or the departure, never on evidence, argument or a reader. Write it the way the CEO would write it in their own notes: the product, the award, the host, the date, the number, in the company's own names. The examples use a fictional company; use this company's names the same way.

## What a title is not

A title is not the evidence, not the argument, not the reader, and not a bare fact. Those read as written by someone outside the company explaining the company to itself.

- **Not the evidence.** Never open with the research that found it: "No public document", "Nothing public shows", "The record shows", "The filings list", "Public sources". A named document may be the subject when the document itself is at issue (the datasheet, the Form Ds, the leadership page).
- **Not the argument.** No clause explaining why it matters. A "because" phrase that names the condition is fine; a clause that persuades is not.
- **Not the reader.** Never name who would notice: no "for a buyer to check", no "and diligence raises it". A buyer or investor may appear only as the holder of the asset ("Investor confidence in the capital story", "GEO sales lost"), never as the watcher.
- **Not a bare fact.** "Capital raised has eight different totals in public" is true and names no risk. "Investor confidence in the capital story, with eight different totals in public" names the asset that is exposed.
- **Not a verdict, and not a lecture.** "Failure to publish results" is a verdict. "The award ends with no follow-on in the record" tells an SBIR company how SBIRs work. Name what is exposed instead.

## The tests, in order

1. **What is the asset?** If the title does not name one, find it in the description and put it first.
2. **What could happen to it?** If the title only states a condition, add the departure. If there is no departure, the row is a finding, and the title stays a plain observation.
3. **Would the founder write it this way?** Read it as a line in the CEO's own list. If it sounds like a report about them, shorten it until it does not.
4. **Is there a tail?** Anything after the departure that explains, hedges or names a reader comes off. A "because" or "with" phrase naming the condition may stay when it fits.
5. **Is it their vocabulary?** Their product, award, host, date and page names. No "the line", "the language", "the record", "the situation", "the exposure".
6. **Is it the normal state of a company like this?** A Phase II that ends, a small team, an unpublished runway: every such company has these. Name the specific asset this company has at stake instead. If there is none, keep the original title and let the description carry the row.
7. **Is it one risk?** Two assets joined by "and" are two rows.
8. **Does it accuse?** "The leadership page dates the founding to both 2017 and 2019" observes. "Inconsistent founding dates undermine credibility" judges. Observe.
9. **Mechanics.** Under 140 characters. No colon, semicolon, em dash, quotation marks or parenthetical. No "X, not Y". No "one of A, B or C"; name the nearest one. No hedging verb unless the row is a scenario, and then one "could" or "can". No relative time (now, currently, recently, this year); write the date or the month. No reference ids or internal codes. No trailing period.

## Examples

Each pair keeps the same facts. The second line is the answer.

Bare fact, no asset:
- Capital raised has eight different totals in public
- Investor confidence in the capital story, with eight different totals in public and no reconciled house figure

Bare fact, no departure:
- The 2022 and 2025 Form Ds list different leadership than the website
- Board changes since 2022 raised in diligence, because the Form Ds and the leadership page list different directors

Condition without the asset it threatens:
- The Halo 6000 digital control path has no named part class
- GEO sales lost to a worst-case radiation assumption, because the Halo 6000 control path names no part class

Evidence recital:
- Three different flight-heritage counts across the launch post, the datasheet and the leadership page
- Heritage claims challenged in a proposal, because the launch post, the datasheet and the leadership page count flights differently

Normal state stated as news:
- ORBIT-2 is the only open federal line, and it ends May 22, 2027
- Continued federal grant revenue after the ORBIT-2 Phase II ends in May 2027

Reader named, provenance hedge:
- No on-orbit figure from the March 2026 Kestrel-3 demonstration is published for a buyer to check
- March 2026 Kestrel-3 demonstration on-orbit data has not been used in marketing

Sources as subject, consequence bolted on:
- The datasheet, ORBIT-2 and the leadership page name three dates no document ties together
- Seed spending planned against three different milestone dates on the datasheet, ORBIT-2 and the leadership page

Abstract subject, literary verb:
- Outside profiles still describe the sensor business the federal record has moved past
- Buyers who screen by profile still find a sensor company

Verdict:
- Failure to publish on-orbit results undermines the efficiency claim
- The efficiency claim rests on two different published figures, one on the announcement and one on the products page

Already right, left alone:
- Halo HV units can corona-fail during chamber pump-down and ascent
- Halo HV units can corona-fail during chamber pump-down and ascent

## When the draft is already good

Return it unchanged. A title that passes every test is not improved by rewording, and a reader who has learned the row by its title should still find it. Change a title only when a test fails.
